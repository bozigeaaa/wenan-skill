# Impact Filter

## One-Point Rule

One script explains one impact point. Do not cover all possible consequences of a hotspot.

Good single points:

- Delivery time uncertainty.
- Freight/insurance cost pressure.
- Port and route planning.
- Procurement lead time.
- Material availability.
- Energy cost sensitivity.
- Camp expansion timing.
- Compliance or safety attention.

Bad combined point:

- "This event affects freight, energy, labor, customs, local factories, insurance, all materials, and project schedules."

## Industry Relevance Test

Keep a hotspot only if it has a clear chain:

```text
hotspot fact
-> operational pathway
-> Saudi project/camp/temporary construction effect
-> one practical takeaway
```

Examples:

- Strait of Hormuz risk -> shipping and insurance uncertainty -> imported materials and project delivery buffers -> procurement should recheck lead time assumptions.
- Oil price spike -> fuel and transport cost sensitivity -> remote-site logistics budgeting -> project managers should separate equipment price from delivery cost.
- New Saudi public-project procurement rule -> supplier qualification boundary -> bid preparation and compliance documents -> do not generalize to all private projects.

Reject if:

- The event is only entertainment, celebrity, or general political drama with no project chain.
- The link to Saudi construction is forced.
- The only usable angle would be product promotion.

## Impact Score

Internally score 0-2 for each:

- Saudi relevance.
- Construction/camp relevance.
- Operational clarity.
- Audience usefulness.
- Fact reliability.

Use only topics scoring 7 or higher out of 10. If no topic qualifies, say no suitable hotspot was found instead of forcing one.

## Default Audience

Write for:

- Project managers.
- Procurement managers.
- Camp managers.
- EPC/general contractor staff.
- Chinese companies working in Saudi or preparing to enter Saudi.

Default takeaway should answer: "What should someone doing Saudi projects recheck because of this?"
