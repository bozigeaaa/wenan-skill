# Professional Knowledge Script Pattern

## Start With the Title Contract

Write one internal sentence before drafting:

> This video answers [the title's exact customer question] with [one verified conclusion].

Every paragraph must earn its place by answering that contract. If removing a paragraph does not weaken the answer, remove it.

## Validation-Only Turns

When the user asks to check, analyze, judge, or confirm the title before generation:

1. State whether the title is viable and under which exact scenario.
2. State the title's one customer question and the one verified conclusion the script would deliver.
3. State the proposed content path and any fact boundary that must not be crossed.
4. Stop and wait for confirmation. Do not include a draft, partial draft, hook, or closing in that turn.

## Choose a Structure, Do Not Copy One

Select the smallest structure that fits the verified problem:

- Mechanism: what controls the result and why.
- Sequence: what happens first, next, and last in the real process.
- Comparison: which conditions make option A or B more suitable.
- Decision rule: what the customer should look at before deciding.
- Checklist: several distinct verified items when the title explicitly asks "what is checked/included."

Do not force a checklist onto every topic. Do not add a "common misunderstanding" unless evidence or the user's scenario shows that misunderstanding is real.

## Opening

- Restate or sharpen the title's exact question in the first sentence.
- Reveal the useful distinction quickly; do not warm up with generic industry commentary.
- Do not insult the viewer's knowledge or invent a widespread mistake.

## Body

- Give concrete verified content, not category labels alone.
- Keep concepts parallel. If one section is an inspection item, do not make the next section an applicant role or document-preparation tip unless the title asks for both.
- Explain why each fact matters to the customer's decision, but do not invent a consequence.
- When using an example, label it as an example and ensure the governing source supports it.

## Ending

- First close the title with the exact practical conclusion.
- Then add one natural interaction prompt tied to the same topic or a genuinely useful next topic.
- Do not switch from inspection items to responsibility, sales, or another approval system in the final sentence.

## Full-Draft Alignment Test

Before output, check:

1. Does the opening ask the same question as the title?
2. Does each paragraph answer that question rather than an adjacent one?
3. Are all listed items the same logical type?
4. Does every concrete claim have direct evidence for this exact scenario?
5. Does the ending resolve the title before inviting interaction?
6. After a revision, did the whole chain remain coherent?

## Avoid

- Starting as科普 but ending as hidden sales pitch.
- Turning a conditional rule into a universal rule.
- Using a regulation name without explaining what it actually controls.
- Automatically writing "first, second, third" before knowing whether the topic has three parallel facts.
- Repeating the title in different words without adding a concrete answer.
- Expanding runtime with responsibility, documents, consequences, or products that the title did not ask about.
